NYMEX
New York Mercantile Exchange - where futures contracts for oil, gasoline, diesel trade in real-time.
Example: WTI crude trades at $82.45/barrel on NYMEX right now
Spread
Price difference between two products, locations, or time periods. Traders profit when spreads widen or narrow.
Example: Midland crude at $82.20, Cushing at $81.00 = $1.20 spread
Z-Score
Measures how unusual a value is. 0 = normal, 2.0 = unusual, 3.0+ = very unusual. Signals strength indicator.
Example: Spread at +2.1 sigma above 60-day average = strong buy signal
MtM (Mark-to-Market)
Current profit/loss on an open position based on today's market prices, not locked-in realized profit.
Example: Bought spread at $1.23, now worth $1.32 = +$90K MtM profit
Precision
Percentage of BUY signals that actually make money. Our system: 63% of signals are profitable.
Example: 100 BUY signals issued, 63 made money, 37 lost money = 63% precision
Sharpe Ratio
Risk-adjusted return. 1.94x means earning $1.94 profit for every $1 of risk taken. Higher = better.
Example: Our system has 1.94x Sharpe ratio (vs S&P500 @ 1.0x)
Contango
Market structure where future prices are HIGHER than spot (current) price. Storage profitable.
Example: Sep WTI = $84, Dec WTI = $86 = Contango of $2
Backwardation
Market structure where future prices are LOWER than spot price. Signals scarcity, urgency.
Example: Sep WTI = $82, Dec WTI = $80 = Backwardation of -$2
Confidence (Model)
How sure the ML model is about its prediction. 95% = very confident, 50% = guessing.
Example: "BUY signal at 82% confidence" = model is fairly sure this is right
Feature
A meaningful data point used by ML model. Example: "Inventory 15% above normal" or "Price moved 2 sigma up".
Example: 100+ features feed into model (price trends, supply, demand, weather, etc.)
Leap Duration
Time horizon for prediction. System predicts spreads 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 days in the future.
Example: 7-day leap = "I predict this spread will be worth X on day 7"
Bid-Ask Spread (Trading Cost)
Cost to execute a trade. Buyer (asks to pay more), Seller (bids lower). Difference is cost.
Example: Bid $1.20, Ask $1.24 = $0.04 cost per barrel to trade
📊 How We Measure Performance (Realistically)
1. Precision (Buy/Sell Accuracy)
Precision = True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives)
What it means: Of all BUY signals, what % actually made money?

How calculated: Count profitable trades ÷ total trades

Our benchmark: 63% (historical)

Why it matters: Traders care most about "how often do you get it right?"
2. Sharpe Ratio (Risk-Adjusted Return)
Sharpe = (Portfolio Return - Risk-Free Rate) / Portfolio Volatility
What it means: How much return per unit of risk taken?

How calculated: Average daily P&L ÷ standard deviation of P&L

Our benchmark: 1.94x (historical)

Why it matters: Rewards systems that make consistent money without wild swings
3. Maximum Drawdown
Max Drawdown = Peak P&L - Trough P&L / Peak P&L
What it means: Worst losing streak. How much profit could you lose?

How calculated: Compare highest peak P&L to lowest point after that

Our limit: 15% maximum (risk management stop-loss)

Why it matters: Protects capital from catastrophic losses
4. Realized P&L vs Model P&L
Realized P&L = Sum of all closed profitable trades
What it means: Actual money in the bank (not unrealized MtM)

How calculated: Only count trades that are closed at a profit

Our benchmark: $13.4M YTD (2025 results)

Why it matters: The only metric traders truly care about
5. Win Rate (Success Ratio)
Win Rate = Winning Trades / Total Trades
What it means: % of trades that made ANY profit (even $1)

How calculated: Count profitable trades ÷ count all trades

Our benchmark: 58% (historical)

Why it matters: Shows consistency, but don't confuse with profit size
6. Average Profit per Trade
Avg Trade P&L = Total P&L / Number of Trades
What it means: How much profit on average per signal executed?

How calculated: Total realized profits ÷ count of trades

Our benchmark: $210K per trade (averaged)

Why it matters: Shows "bang for buck" - trade efficiency
7. Model Drift Detection
Precision Last 30 Days vs Historical Average
What it means: Is the model getting worse? When to retrain?

How calculated: Compare recent precision to baseline precision

Alert threshold: >5% drop triggers automatic retraining

Why it matters: Catches when market conditions change and models need updating
8. Signal Latency
Time from Market Change → Signal Generated → Trader Sees It
What it means: How fast from data arrival to trader seeing signal?

How calculated: Timestamp market data received minus timestamp signal published

Our benchmark: <10 seconds for futures, <5 minutes for physical

Why it matters: Traders lose $ if signals arrive after opportunities pass
⚡ NYMEX Futures Prices
What It Does
Real-time trading prices for oil, gasoline, diesel futures contracts traded on the New York Mercantile Exchange.
Why It's Important
NYMEX is the global benchmark for US oil prices. Every trader watches it. Our system uses tick-by-tick data (every second) to detect price moves and generate signals.
How It's Used
  • Features: Current price, moving averages, volatility, Z-scores
  • Frequency: Every second during market hours (9:30am-3:00pm EST + after-hours)
  • In predictions: "Is WTI trending up or down? How volatile?"
Business Example
WTI drops $2 in 1 hour, Z-score hits -2.5 (unusual). Model says "buy now, it will rebound." Trader executes. Price rebounds to $81.50. +$150K profit.
🚢 Kpler Physical Flows
What It Does
Real-time tracking of tanker movements carrying oil globally. Not predictions—actual ships, actual cargo.
Why It's Important
Physical flows are early signal of demand/supply. If oil is flowing from Midland → Houston, it suggests Midland has excess (prices might drop there). Kpler data is more real than EIA reports (weekly).
How It's Used
  • Features: Daily barrel flows by region, pipeline utilization, tanker count
  • Frequency: Daily updates, some real-time tracking
  • In predictions: "Is supply heading to oversupply or shortage regions?"
Business Example
Kpler shows 500K barrels/day flows from Midland to Gulf (normal 300K). Means oversupply building. Model predicts Midland-Cushing spread will widen. Short spread before prices adjust. Profit when they do.
📋 EIA (Energy Information Administration)
What It Does
Official US government energy data. Weekly reports on inventory levels, refinery runs, production, imports/exports.
Why It's Important
EIA reports move markets. "Inventory jumped 5 million barrels unexpectedly" → prices drop. Our model learns these patterns and predicts how much prices will move before reports hit.
How It's Used
  • Features: Weekly inventory levels, surprise % vs forecast, 5-year seasonal norms
  • Frequency: Wednesday morning weekly releases
  • In predictions: "Is supply above/below normal? By how much?"
Business Example
Model predicts "EIA inventory will surprise 8% below forecast." Day before release, model buys at $80. Report shows -8M barrels (surprise!). Price jumps to $82.50. Trader sells. +$2.50/barrel profit.
🏭 Internal Operations Data
What It Does
Marathon's own data: refinery production forecasts (MPR/MIPS), transportation costs (TRR), maintenance schedules (Planning DB).
Why It's Important
Only Marathon has this data. Competitors can't see it. Refinery turnarounds change production. We know about them. That's competitive advantage.
How It's Used
  • MPR/MIPS: Forecast crude runs, product output. "Refinery down 50K bbl/day next week"
  • TRR: Transportation costs change. Affects product basis values
  • Planning DB: Turnarounds, maintenance windows. Supply timing
Business Example
Planning DB shows: "Midland refinery down for maintenance Aug 15-20 (50K bbl/day)." Model predicts RBOB will tighten (less gasoline production). Buys RBOB spreads now. When maintenance starts, spreads widen. $1.2M profit.
📦 Bronze Layer (Raw Data Lake)
What It Does
Stores raw, unprocessed data from all 18 sources. Exact copy of what came in. No cleaning, no transformation.
Why It's Important
Immutable audit trail. If a prediction is wrong, we trace back to raw data to debug. Also, if we later discover a better way to clean data, we can reprocess from Bronze.
Technical Details
  • Storage: Delta Lake (Apache Spark format), Azure cloud
  • Organization: Partitioned by ingestion_date and source
  • Retention: 7+ years of history
  • Size: ~2TB daily ingestion
Example Flow
Raw NYMEX tick data (18GB/day) → Bronze Layer. Stores as-is. Later, Silver Layer cleans duplicates, fixes data types. Gold Layer computes features.
🔧 Silver Layer (Cleaned Data)
What It Does
Cleans and standardizes raw data. Removes duplicates, fixes missing values, converts units (e.g., all prices to USD/barrel), aligns timestamps across sources.
Why It's Important
ML models need clean data. Garbage in = garbage out. Silver Layer ensures all data is consistent, no duplicates, no nulls breaking the pipeline.
Transformations
  • Remove duplicate ticks (same price, same timestamp)
  • Handle nulls: forward-fill or interpolate based on context
  • Convert all prices to USD/barrel (standardize units)
  • Align timezones to UTC
  • Validate data types (price is float, not string)
Organization
Partitioned by trade_date and instrument_id. Fast queries: "Get all WTI prices on 2026-08-09"
✨ Gold Layer (Features & Aggregates)
What It Does
Pre-computed features ready for ML models. 100+ features: spreads, moving averages, Z-scores, supply/demand signals, seasonal adjustments.
Why It's Important
ML models score 10x faster if features are pre-computed. Instead of computing on-the-fly (slow), we materialize nightly, models just look up values.
Feature Categories
  • Price Series: 1h, 1d, 1w OHLCV bars
  • Technical: MA, RSI, Bollinger, Vol, Z-score
  • Spreads: Time (F1-F3), Geo (Midland-Cushing), Crack (3:2:1)
  • Fundamentals: Inventory surprise, seasonal norms, supply/demand changes
Example
Gold Layer stores: "WTI on 2026-08-09: 7d MA = $81.20, Z-score = +1.8, inventory 15% above normal, seasonal norm +5%."

Model retrieves these instantly, makes prediction without recomputing.
🎯 Feature Store (100+ Computed Features)
What It Does
Central repository of 100+ pre-computed features. ML models query this store to get feature values for scoring.
Why It's Important
Single source of truth. Data scientists can build models using same features. Ensures training & production use exact same values. No "training/serving skew" bugs.
Feature Examples
  • wti_7d_moving_avg: 7-day average price
  • spread_midland_cushing_zscore: How unusual is current spread?
  • inventory_surprise_pct: EIA vs forecast difference
  • seasonal_adjustment: How does this compare to same week last 5 years?
  • refinery_utilization: How many barrels/day can refineries process?
Platform
Databricks Feature Store. Integrated with MLflow. Models point to feature definitions, not hard-code values. Allows easy updates.
🧠 LightGBM Models (50+ Regressors)
What It Does
Machine learning models that predict spread values at future dates. Given 100+ feature values, outputs: "This spread will be $1.05 in 7 days."
Why It's Important
Replaces manual analysis. ML learns patterns from 5+ years of data. Predicts better than human guesswork. 63% accuracy historically.
Model Structure
  • Type: LightGBM (Gradient Boosting regression)
  • Count: 50+ models (per commodity × leap duration)
  • Training: 5+ years historical data, walk-forward validation
  • Update: Weekly retraining, daily inference
Example: 7-Day WTI Spread Model
Input: 100 features (price, supply, demand, weather, macro)
Output: "Midland-Cushing spread: prediction = $0.95"
Confidence: R² = 0.71 (explains 71% of variance)
📊 MLflow Model Registry
What It Does
Centralized hub for tracking, versioning, and deploying ML models. Each model has version history, performance metrics, training data info.
Why It's Important
Prevents "model chaos." Without it: which version is in production? Which performed best? MLflow tracks everything.
Tracked Metadata
  • Model version (v1.0, v1.1, v2.0)
  • Training date and data window
  • Performance metrics (R², RMSE, MAE)
  • Backtesting Sharpe ratio
  • Production deployment status
Usage
API server loads model: "Give me v1.3 of WTI-7day model." MLflow returns trained model from registry. Inference happens instantly.
🎯 Signal Orchestration Engine
What It Does
Takes 50+ model predictions, merges them, applies risk filters, ranks by expected profit. Output: "Top 20 trades to execute NOW."
Why It's Important
Raw predictions are useless without decision logic. Orchestration adds business rules: risk limits, conflicts, ranking.
Process
  • Merge: Combine signals from 1d, 3d, 7d, 30d models for same commodity
  • Thresholds: Convert regression values to BUY/SELL/NEUTRAL
  • Risk Filters: Don't exceed position limits, don't overlap trades
  • Confidence: Higher if multiple timeframes agree
  • Ranking: Sort by expected profit and risk-adjusted return
Example
1d model: BUY @ 70%, 7d model: BUY @ 80%, 30d model: NEUTRAL @ 55%
Orchestration merges: "BUY signal" at 68% confidence (2-of-3 agree)
📖 Signal Book (Ranked Trade Ideas)
What It Does
Final output: ranked list of 20 best trade ideas, with entry prices, exit prices, expected profit, confidence.
Why It's Important
This is what traders actually execute. Everything before is pipeline setup. Signal Book is the delivered product.
Data Stored
  • Signal ID (unique)
  • Commodity (WTI, RBOB, etc.)
  • Strategy (time arb, geo arb, crack spread)
  • Signal (BUY/SELL/NEUTRAL)
  • Entry price & date
  • Exit price & date (predicted)
  • Expected profit
  • Confidence %
  • Features that drove it
Storage
PostgreSQL database. Fast queries. Updates real-time as new signals generated. Dashboard queries it continuously.

Backend server that serves data to React dashboard. Exposes 9+ endpoints (signals, spreads, forecasts, P&L, etc.).
Why It's Important
Dashboard needs fast data. API caches hot data, serves to traders instantly. WebSocket for real-time updates.
Key Endpoints
  • GET /signals/latest → Top 20 signals
  • GET /spreads/{commodity} → Historical + forecast
  • GET /forecasts/{commodity} → Model predictions
  • GET /pnl/attribution → Profits by product
  • WS /ws/signals → Real-time stream
Performance

Panel 1: Signals Overview
What You're Seeing
A table of active signals across all commodities. Each row shows:
• Commodity: What product (WTI, RBOB, etc.)
• Contract: Which month/spread
• Signal: BUY (green), SELL (red), NEUTRAL (gray)
• Confidence: How sure the model is (0-100%)
How It's Calculated
Signal: Regression prediction → Compare to threshold
Confidence: Agreement across models + prediction magnitude
Example: If 7d model predicts spread will be $1.05 (current $1.23), and 30d also agrees, confidence rises.
How To Use It
Sort by confidence (descending). Focus on signals >70% confidence first. Lower confidence (<55%) might wait.
Panel 2: Spread View
What You're Seeing
A time-series chart showing spread history and prediction:
• Gray line: Historical spread prices (past)
• Colored line: Model forecast (future)
• Vertical line: Today (divides past/future)
How It's Calculated
Historical: Price of Product A minus Price of Product B (e.g., Midland WTI - Cushing WTI)
Forecast: LightGBM prediction of spread value at future date
Example: Spread today = $1.23. Model predicts $0.95 in 7 days.
How To Use It
If line slopes down (forecast < current), spread will narrow. That's a SHORT opportunity (sell now, buy back cheaper). If slopes up, it's a LONG.
Panel 3: Market Signal Details
What You're Seeing
The trade idea with specific prices:
• Entry Date & Price: When to buy and at what price
• Exit Date & Price: When to sell (prediction) and at what price
• Expected Profit: $ per barrel (if prediction is right)
• Bid-Ask Cost: Transaction cost (subtracted from profit)
How It's Calculated
Entry Price: Current market price (from NYMEX)
Exit Price: LightGBM prediction at future date
Profit: (Exit - Entry) × Quantity - Bid-Ask Cost
Example: Buy at $1.23, sell at $0.95 = -$0.28 loss? NO! You're SHORT (selling first). Sell at $1.23, buy back at $0.95 = +$0.28 profit!
How To Use It
This is THE trade to execute. Entry/exit prices are specific, not just "buy low sell high" guesswork.
Panel 4: Forecast Distribution
What You're Seeing
A bell curve showing probability distribution of spread values.
• Peak: Most likely value (model's best guess)
• Tails: Less likely but possible outcomes
• Green zone: Profitable prices (you make money here)
• Red zone: Loss prices (you lose money here)
How It's Calculated
Distribution: Model outputs spread prediction + uncertainty estimate
Confidence Interval: 80% chance spread lands within this range
Example: 80% CI = $0.78 to $1.12. 10% chance >$1.12, 10% chance <$0.78
How To Use It
If entry at $1.23 and entire profitable zone is $0.78-$1.12, risk > reward. Pass. If profitable zone is $1.50-$2.00 and you can buy at $1.23, good risk/reward. Execute.
Panel 5: Feature Impact
What You're Seeing
A waterfall chart showing which factors influenced the model's prediction.
• Top bar: Strongest impact (e.g., +45% from inventory surprise)
• Lower bars: Weaker impacts (e.g., +9% from macro factors)
Total adds up to 100% (the full signal)
How It's Calculated
SHAP values: Machine learning technique that shows each feature's contribution
Example: Inventory surprise = +45%, Momentum = +28%, Seasonal = +18%, Macro = +9%
If inventory surprised in the past, spreads typically narrowed. Model learned this. That's the +45%.
How To Use It
Build confidence. If signal is 45% from "inventory" and you know inventory is indeed surprising, trust it more. If 45% from an obscure macro indicator you don't track, be skeptical.
Panel 6: P&L Attribution
What You're Seeing
Total profits generated by the system, broken down by product.
• Realized P&L: Actual money in the bank (only from closed trades)
• By product: How much profit from WTI vs RBOB vs Diesel spreads
• Total: Sum of all profits
How It's Calculated
Realized P&L: For each closed trade: (Exit Price - Entry Price) × Quantity - Transaction Costs
By Product: Sum all trades for that product
Example: 50 WTI spread trades closed. 32 made money (+$2.3M total). 18 lost money (-$0.0M total). Net = +$2.3M.
How To Use It
This is the ONLY metric that matters. All the fancy features don't mean anything if profits are zero. Watch this religiously.
